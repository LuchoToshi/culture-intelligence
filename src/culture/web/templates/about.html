ABOUT

Evidence first. Trends are what's left afterwards.

This platform tracks how tastes, products, places and cultural identities form and spread across influential cities — by monitoring what the culture actually produces, connecting the evidence, and refusing to call one sighting a movement.

Who it's for

Strategy, trend and creative teams at brands, retailers and agencies — people who need to know what is genuinely moving before it is obvious, and who need to see why a claim is being made, not just the claim.

How it works

01 · OBSERVE
A curated network of publications, creators and cultural communities across Europe, the UK, the US, Japan, Korea and Australia is monitored continuously.
02 · ANALYZE
Each piece of content is analyzed for what it claims, what it shows, and what it implies — with source claims kept strictly separate from our interpretation.
03 · CORROBORATE
Observations become signals only by recurring across independent observers. One account saying something twice is one observer's view, not confirmation.
04 · SYNTHESIZE
Signals are tracked over time — strengthening, saturating, declining — and written up weekly as the Signal report and as essays in The Brief.

Why we never name our sources

The monitored network is the product's edge, so public output presents synthesized insight, not inputs: what was observed, how often, across how many independent observers, and how recently. Descriptors stay generic — "a Tokyo subculture publication", "several independent street-style accounts". The brands, artists, cities and products the culture is about are always named; it is the observers that stay anonymous.

How to read our numbers

Every public claim carries its evidence shape: observation count, independent-source count, and freshness. That is deliberately checkable structure — you can see how much weight a claim carries without us revealing who carried it.

Limitations, stated plainly

Coverage follows the network: under-covered cities may simply be under-observed, and the platform says so on its own pages. Classification of early signals is provisional until corroborated. Analysis is model-assisted and human-directed; where a judgment is editorial, it is labeled as one. No personal data about private individuals is collected — the evidence is published culture, not people's accounts.
